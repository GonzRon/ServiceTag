# ServiceTag 1.9.0

ServiceTag 1.9.0 (version code 23) is a feature release. An asset can now tell Home Assistant two facts about itself: its equipment status, and the date of its Season Start service, the service that makes it ready for its season. A schedule can wait for that service before it starts counting, and one schedule can follow a Home Assistant season of its own. The Assets list shows which assets are linked to Home Assistant, which are in season and what condition each is in, and it can filter by condition. Three fixes that were to be 1.8.2 ship here too: a form schedule's meter reading, form buttons under the keyboard, and a reminder left behind after a completion. It installs in place over 1.8.1, and the database and backups move one step forward at first launch.

ServiceTag reports facts; Home Assistant decides what to do with them. Nothing is written to Home Assistant until you turn publishing on for an asset, and then only to the helper you chose.

## Equipment status in Home Assistant (#113)

On an asset's page, **Publish equipment status to Home Assistant** sends the asset's recorded condition to a dropdown helper (`input_select`) that your Home Assistant administrator created, whose options are exactly `UP`, `DOWN` and `DEGRADED`: Operational is sent as `UP`, Degraded as `DEGRADED` and Down as `DOWN`. **Choose helper** lists the dropdowns, or you enter the entity ID by hand. The phone reads the helper once and refuses one that does not fit, saying why. Then it names the exact destination and what will be sent — "Publish Example Heater's equipment status to input_select.example_heater_equipment_status? …" — and nothing is written before **Publish**.

The status is the current condition alone: no health, due date, completion, season or readiness reaches it. With no condition recorded, nothing is sent ("Waiting: no equipment status is recorded, so nothing is sent."); ServiceTag never invents an `UP`. After each write the phone reads the helper back, and it says "Published" only when Home Assistant holds what it sent. **Publish now** reads the helper and writes where Home Assistant differs, **Check helper** checks that the helper still fits, and **Stop publishing** asks first; Home Assistant keeps the value it has.

The phone sends when it can: at once while the app runs and its network rule allows it, when you open or return to ServiceTag, on **Publish now**, and in the background where allowed. It is not instant, and Home Assistant receives the current state, not every change in between.

## The Season Start service (#114)

One schedule can be an asset's **Season Start service**: the service that makes it ready for its season. On a schedule's page, **Use as Season Start service** asks first and sets **Services count from**, today unless you choose an earlier day; only completions dated on or after it count. **Stop using as Season Start service** clears it. It covers an asset with seasons — a calendar or a manual season, never year-round — that has no parent, or has child assets of its own. A group's schedule can be the Season Start service of every asset in the group: each member is ready from its own completion, and one member's service never counts for another.

The asset is **ready for the season** exactly when that schedule has a completion of this asset, its details filled in, dated on or after the later of two days: the **Services count from** day and the day the asset's season last ended. ServiceTag reads nothing else for it: no condition, health, reminder or due date, and no name or category. The season card says "Ready for the season · serviced …", or why not — for example "Not ready: not serviced since the season ended on …" — and where that day came from.

- A schedule completed with one tap counts only when you say so, with **Count one-tap completions**.
- A service brought in by a Transfer Pack never counts for the asset that received it: record this season's service on this phone. A ServiceTag backup of your own, restored or merged, counts as recorded here.
- A schedule's completion can no longer be dated after today, on the phone, the Developer API or the MCP: "The date cannot be later than today." An older record dated ahead keeps its date and does not count until it is edited on or after that day.

**Publish Season Start date to Home Assistant** sends the service date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) to a date helper (`input_datetime`, with a date and no time) while the asset is ready for the season, and **`1970-01-01`** otherwise. `1970-01-01` means not ready: no Season Start service, no qualifying service since the season ended, the service deleted, the role removed, or the asset archived, retired, transferred out or deleted. Your Home Assistant rule must treat it as not serviced. On a season Home Assistant drives, the publication also names a season-ended helper, which the phone reads and never writes.

**Letting go of a date helper.** **Stop publishing** first sets the helper to `1970-01-01`, so Home Assistant stops treating the old date as a service, and so does every other way ServiceTag lets go of one: another helper chosen, the asset archived, retired, transferred out or deleted, or Disconnect. Until Home Assistant reads `1970-01-01` back, the helper is still owed it: the block says "Not yet cleared in Home Assistant: …" and ServiceTag keeps trying. When it can no longer reach the helper, it says so and asks you to set it to `1970-01-01` in Home Assistant yourself. Do that, then tap **I cleared it in Home Assistant**: only that explicit acknowledgement ends what is owed. A helper whose asset is gone is listed on the Home Assistant screen.

The setup — the helpers, the season-ended helper and the start rule on Home Assistant's side — is in [Home Assistant: season sync and publishing](../home-assistant-season-sync.md).

## Counting from the Season Start service (#124)

Weekly checks that only make sense once the season's opening service is done can now wait for it. A schedule's **Start counting from** can be **The Season Start service**, with **Days after the Season Start service**. In season and not yet serviced, the schedule is **Waiting** ("Waiting for this season's Season Start service"), with no date, no reminder and no effect on health. Once the asset is ready, it counts from the later of the service day and the season's start, plus the days after, so a late service moves the checks with it ("Moved from … to count from the Season Start service"). Out of season it behaves as "When the season starts".

It is offered on an asset's own schedules with a time rule, on a calendar or a manual season, never on a group's schedule and never on the Season Start service itself. While a schedule counts from it, the Season Start service cannot be cleared or archived: the phone names the schedule that counts from it and says "Change when it starts counting first."

## A schedule's own Home Assistant season (#125)

One schedule can now follow a Home Assistant season of its own while its asset keeps its own season. On the schedule's page, the season row reads "Season: Example Heater's season" with **Follow Home Assistant**: choose an on/off helper (`input_boolean`), such as `input_boolean.example_heater_service_window`, and that schedule comes due by the helper's season. Only that schedule's due date moves. The asset's season, its other schedules, its Season Start readiness and everything it publishes keep reading the asset's season. It is Follow only, with no Force, over the same connection, token, network rule and interval as an asset's link. **Use the asset's season** asks first, returns the schedule to the asset's season and keeps its own season history.

A group's schedule follows no season of its own. The schedule's own season rows travel in every backup, merge and Transfer Pack; the link stays on this phone.

## The Assets list (#116)

Each row on the Assets list now shows:

- the asset's **condition** — Operational, Degraded or Down — on every asset in service with a condition recorded;
- an **in-season** mark when an asset with seasons is in season. An asset out of season keeps its "Out of season" badge, and a year-round asset shows neither;
- a **Home Assistant** mark when the asset is linked to Home Assistant: its season follows a helper, or it publishes its equipment status or its Season Start date.

The two marks sit to the left of the NFC mark and are read out as "In season" and "Linked to Home Assistant". A new **Condition** filter, beside Type, shows all assets or only those that are Operational, Degraded or Down. Nothing new is stored, and the choice is not saved, like the list's other filters.

## Fixes (#119, #121, #123)

- **A form schedule's meter reading (#119).** A schedule that uses a form and also counts a meter now asks for the meter reading on its form, and the form saves nothing without it. In 1.8.1 the form took the reading only if its profile carried it.
- **The keyboard (#121).** The window now resizes for the keyboard instead of sliding up, and the status and navigation bars are counted once, not twice. A form's buttons, **Record entry** among them, no longer sit under the keyboard while you type.
- **A reminder left behind (#123).** Completing a schedule from its page, from a group's page (**Complete all**, **Complete selected**), from a reminder's **Done** that opens the app, or from the **Log maintenance** list now clears its reminder at once. Before, the reminder stayed in the notification shade until the next reminder check.

## Developer API & MCP

The Developer API and the MCP tools read and drive the new pieces, never the secrets: an asset's season readiness and the Season Start role, Home Assistant helper discovery, publishing with its plan and its controls, the acknowledgement of a helper still owed `1970-01-01` (only with the owner's own word), and a schedule's own season. No route and no tool takes a value, a date to publish or a ready flag: what is published is always worked out from the phone's own records. There are now 32 sub-resources under `/v1/assets/{id}/`, and the MCP server has 104 tools. The new tools refuse a phone running an older ServiceTag before sending anything.

**One thing a script could meet:** a schedule's completion dated after the phone's today — on `POST /v1/schedules/{id}/complete`, or a `PATCH /v1/events/{id}` of one — is refused with 422 `COMPLETION_DATE_IN_FUTURE`, and nothing is written. An ordinary event keeps any date.

## Upgrading

- 1.9.0 installs in place over 1.8.1, and your data stays.
- The database moves to schema 22 and backups to format 21, in one step at first launch. A 1.8.x export restores into 1.9.0. 1.8.x refuses a backup or a Transfer Pack made by 1.9.0 as too new, rather than dropping what it cannot read. Take an export before upgrading.
- Nothing is published until you turn it on for an asset, and no schedule is a Season Start service until you make it one. The Home Assistant connection and its token are untouched.
- The connection, the season links and the publications are this phone's own: no backup, export, merge or Transfer Pack carries them. The Season Start role and a schedule's own season rows travel as data.
- A schedule's completion already dated after today keeps its date; it does not count for the Season Start service until it is edited on or after that day.

## Known limits

- The nine translations of 1.9.0's new sentences were written without a native speaker's review.
- A Transfer Pack round trip does not carry a schedule's own Home Assistant season link, just as it does not carry an asset's (#16): the schedule comes back "No longer linked to Home Assistant" and is linked again by hand.
- The phone has no **Change target** for publishing: stop publishing, then turn it on again with the new helper. The Developer API has the change in one step.
- A schedule following its own Home Assistant season has no predicted start date ("Out of season until you start it"). The Assets list's Home Assistant mark is the asset's own link, not a schedule's.
- ServiceTag reports facts — the equipment status and the service date — and Home Assistant decides what to do with them. Whether and when the equipment runs is decided by your Home Assistant automations, never by ServiceTag.
- The keyboard check across every form was not repeated for 1.9.0, by the owner's ruling; the insets tests and the connected suite cover it.

The Developer API's contracts are in [`docs/api/v1.md`](../api/v1.md).
