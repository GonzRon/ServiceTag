# ServiceTag 1.5.0

ServiceTag 1.5.0 (version code 18) is a feature release. It adds lending, Transfer Packs, Replace asset and Save as document, and a tap on a maintenance notification now opens the right screen. It installs in place over 1.4.1.

## Lending (#72)

You can now lend an asset out. On the asset's page, the **Lending** section has **Lend out**: choose the borrower from your Contacts — a person or an organisation — set the day it was lent and, if you like, the day it is due back. ServiceTag does not ask for access to your contacts; it reads only the one contact you pick, once.

While an asset is lent out, its page and the Assets list say **Lent out**, with who has it and when it is due back. Once the due date has passed, it reads **Loan overdue** and appears on the Dashboard. A loan can remind you **Once**, when it is due back, or **Until returned**, every day until you mark it returned. Loan reminders have their own notification channel and are never counted as overdue maintenance.

When it comes back, tap **Mark returned**. The loan stays on the asset under **Past loans**. Lending never changes an asset's condition, schedules or service record. Loans travel in backups and merges, and the Developer API and the MCP tools can read and record them.

## Transfer Packs (#77)

For equipment that changes hands for good — selling a house and leaving some equipment behind, for example — choose **Transfer assets** from the Assets list or an asset's menu. Select what is leaving (components go with the asset they belong to), review what the pack will contain, add a note for the new owner if you like, and tap **Create Transfer Pack**. The pack holds those assets' records, schedules, documents and photos, service cases and NFC tag identities, so the same tags keep working on the new owner's phone. **Share** it, or **Save a copy**.

ServiceTag then asks whether to mark those assets transferred out on this phone. Marked assets are archived: their reminders stop, they leave your lists and they are left out of later backups, but you can still open them under Archive to read their history. Scanning one of their tags says the asset was handed over.

On the other phone, **Import Transfer Pack** on the Backup screen shows what the pack contains and adds it without replacing anything already there. If an asset you transferred out comes back, a Transfer Pack made on the other phone brings it home; an old backup cannot. If a Transfer Pack never actually left, its record can be withdrawn from the page of any asset in it: the withdrawal covers the whole pack, and its assets stay archived.

An asset that is lent out has to be marked returned before it can be transferred.

## Replace asset (#86)

When a piece of equipment is replaced — a new water heater in place of the old one, say — choose **Replace asset** from the old asset's menu. In one step the old asset is retired and a new, separate asset is created, and each page links to the other: "Replaced by …" on the old one, "Replaces …" on the new one. Tap either to open the other.

The old asset keeps its identity, history, documents and service record exactly as they were; nothing is copied from its history. You choose what carries forward, and every item starts unticked: schedules (one at a time, each with a start date you review), readings and actions, the operating season and maintenance break, group memberships, and the description and notes. If you carry forward a manually started season, ServiceTag asks whether the new asset was in season on the replacement date, with no answer chosen for you.

NFC tags stay with the old asset unless you choose **Move** for a tag. A moved tag is not rewritten; scanning it opens the new asset. The replacement date cannot be later than today, and if anything changed while you were reviewing, ServiceTag refuses the replacement and says so.

There is no undo yet: a replacement cannot be unlinked, and deleting either asset removes the link. The Developer API and the MCP tools can read a replacement but cannot make one.

## Save as document (#85)

An asset's web links can now become real documents. On an https link, the ⋮ menu offers **Save as document**, between Open and Edit. ServiceTag downloads the file and checks from its contents — not its name — that it matches one of the supported formats:

- PDF
- PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP
- RTF
- DOC, XLS, PPT (Word, Excel and PowerPoint, older format)
- DOCX, XLSX, PPTX (Word, Excel and PowerPoint, current format)
- ODT, ODS, ODP (OpenDocument text, spreadsheet and presentation)
- TXT, Markdown, CSV, TSV

It then shows you where the file came from, what it is and how big it is. Adjust the name, kind, role or notes, tap Save, and it becomes an ordinary document on the same asset, in your attachments folder. The link stays exactly where it was and now reads "Saved as document". The document remembers its source ("Downloaded from … on …", with an **Open source link** button in its edit sheet), and that record travels with backups, merges and Transfer Packs. Once the save itself has started, it finishes even if you leave the screen.

A login page, an error page or any other web page is never saved as a document. Neither is anything over 256 MB, a link that is not https, or a link that leads into your own network. The download sends the link and nothing else: no cookies, no sign-in, and no identifying details beyond the name "ServiceTag". It runs only while the sheet is open; Cancel stops it and leaves nothing behind. This is ServiceTag's first outbound network use, and it happens only when you tap. If network access is turned off for ServiceTag, the sheet says so and offers the app settings.

## Maintenance notifications open the right screen (#87)

Tapping a maintenance notification now takes you somewhere: the summary ("… maintenance items need attention") opens the Dashboard, where ATTENTION is the first section, and a single item's notification opens that schedule, the same as its Open button. A tap only opens a screen — it never marks anything done, snoozes it or changes a date.

While you are adding or editing an asset, replacing one, or making or importing a Transfer Pack, tapping the summary just brings ServiceTag back to where you were.

This applies to notifications ServiceTag posts after you install 1.5.0. A maintenance notification that was already showing when you installed 1.5.0 still does nothing when its body is tapped, until ServiceTag next re-posts it.

## Also in 1.5.0

Small fixes (#84):

- After importing a Transfer Pack from the Backup screen, you come back to the Backup screen, which says what was imported.
- Marking assets transferred out always ends on the Assets list.
- If a Transfer Pack's file is gone before you share it, ServiceTag says so and asks you to create it again.
- The Dashboard's overdue-loan row names the parent asset when the lent asset is a component of another.
- A document added from the asset editor takes its kind from its role: a receipt is filed as a receipt, a manual as a manual.
- A loan's Once reminder rings once, and only once.

Work merged after 1.4.1 that reaches a release for the first time here:

- Asset categories are kept as a catalog that the editor and backups carry (#74).
- The Assets list gains type, components and archived filters (#73), and health and NFC indicators on its rows (#71).
- Documents can carry a role, with key documents and intake from the asset editor (#67).
- Incident and condition workflows over the service record (#82).
- Warranty status, a reminder before a warranty ends, and service and warranty cases (#79).
- The asset editor asks about year-round schedules when an asset becomes seasonal (#78).
- The tag-overwrite sheet names what the tag already identifies (#70).
- The quick-action grid no longer clips long labels (#68).
- The Developer API's refusals name the first problem and its field, and its screen says why its listener is not running (#52, #51, #66); the MCP tools' schedule update keeps reminders in step (#83).

## Upgrading

- 1.5.0 installs in place over 1.4.1, and your data stays.
- The database moves to schema 16 and backups to format 16. Older backups still import — restore and merge — as before.
- An older ServiceTag, 1.4.1 or earlier, refuses a 1.5.0 backup rather than dropping what it cannot read.
- Maintenance notifications that were already showing when you installed 1.5.0 keep a body that does nothing when tapped, until ServiceTag next re-posts them. 1.5.0 does not refresh them.
- Save as document is ServiceTag's one outbound network use: https only, and only when you tap it. It uses the network permission ServiceTag already had for the Developer API.

## Known limits

- Save as document checks that a file's contents match a supported format. It is not a malware scan and not a full check of the document; ServiceTag never runs a downloaded file.
- Macro-enabled Office files (DOCM, XLSM, PPTM) are refused.
- While one of the four protected screens is open — adding or editing an asset, replacing one, making a Transfer Pack or importing one — tapping the maintenance summary only brings ServiceTag to the front; it does not open the Dashboard.
- Maintenance notifications already showing from 1.4.1 keep an inert body until ServiceTag re-posts them.
