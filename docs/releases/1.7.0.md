# ServiceTag 1.7.0

ServiceTag 1.7.0 (version code 20) is a feature release. It adds a catalog of the supplies your equipment takes, a record of what is fitted inside an asset and what replaced it, files and links on both, and an operating season that can follow Home Assistant. It installs in place over 1.6.0 and over 1.5.0.

## Supply items (#15)

Maintenance now has a **Supplies** list: one entry for each product your equipment takes, such as a filter cartridge, a battery, a belt or a chemical. A supply item has a name, a category, the manufacturer, the model, a **Part number**, a preferred unit, notes, and the specifications you want to keep, each a label, a value and a unit. A supply item you no longer use is archived, never deleted, and you can bring it back.

On an asset, the new **Supplies** section says which supply items the asset takes and in what role ("Oil filter", "Replacement battery"). An asset can take several supply items, and one supply item can serve several assets; a supply item's own page lists them under **Used by**. Add one with **Add supply**, change its role with **Edit role**, or remove it; removing it changes nothing else.

On a quick action's **Materials**, **Link supply** ties a line to a supply item and **Remove link** takes it off. A journal entry started from that quick action keeps the link, shown as "Linked to …". The line keeps its own name and unit as you wrote them, and ServiceTag never links a line because its name matches a supply item: a link is there only because you chose it. Replacing something on a schedule uses the same schedules, quick actions and journal entries as before; a schedule completed with only a date records no materials, so it names no supply item. Supply items, their roles on your assets and the links on material lines travel in backups, merges and Transfer Packs.

## Installed components (#47)

An asset can now record what is fitted inside it: the battery tray, the pack in it, a membrane in its housing. Each one is an installed component, listed in the asset's new **Installed components** section, and one can sit inside another with **Install inside**. An installed component has a name, the date it was installed, a **Serial or lot**, notes and, if you want, the supply item it is and what it is made of: a **Composition** of supply items with quantities, such as "4 × Example 12 V Battery".

**Replace** puts a new unit in the same place on a date, and **Remove** takes one out. Either way the old one stays as history (**Removed** under the section shows them), and everything installed inside it is removed on the same date; nothing is deleted. The new unit starts with nothing inside it, and with only the supply item and composition you give it. **Edit component** corrects the name, the supply item, the composition, the serial or lot, the install date and the notes; a removal date is set once.

An installed component is not an asset: it has no NFC tag and no journal or schedules of its own. What the phone used to call **Components**, assets that belong to another asset, are now called **Child assets**: on asset detail, in the Assets tab's control, on the Dashboard and in Transfer Packs, so the two never share a word. Child assets work exactly as before. Installed components travel in backups, merges and Transfer Packs, their history included.

## Files and links on supply items and installed components (#69)

Files and links can now belong to a supply item or to an installed component, not only to an asset. Each file or link has exactly one owner, fixed for life: an asset, a supply item or an installed component, or, for a file, a journal entry. These files and links travel in backups, restores, merges and Transfer Packs like an asset's own.

A supply item's page has its own **Documents** and **References**: its manual, its data sheet, a photo of its package, the maker's page. They are kept once. Every installed component that names the supply item shows them under "From …", to open there and to add or change on the supply item itself.

An installed component's **Documents and references** opens its own screen. **This installed component** holds what is about that one fitted unit, such as an installation photo, its label or its wiring, and below it come the files and links of the supply items it names. **Save as document** works on a supply item's or an installed component's web link exactly as on an asset's.

Sharing a link, a document or a photo into ServiceTag can now save it to any of the three. **Attach to** chooses **Assets** (the default), **Installed components** or **Supplies**, each with its own search, and tapping an asset offers **This asset**, its supply items and the installed components inside it. Saved to a supply item, the form says it will be available wherever that supply is used. A shared note still goes to an asset. The asset list when you share now offers only assets maintained here: archived, retired and transferred-out assets are not offered, so it has no **Archived** control.

## Home Assistant season sync (#16)

An asset's operating season can now follow one Home Assistant on/off helper, such as a heater's heating season set by your own automation. It does nothing until you connect: Settings › Utilities › **Home Assistant** takes your Home Assistant's address and a long-lived access token from a Home Assistant user made for ServiceTag, without administrator rights. Then **Link to Home Assistant** on an asset's season card names the helper; the sheet says what linking does to the asset's season before anything is written.

When the phone checks, `on` starts the season and `off` ends it, through the same Start season and End season you could tap yourself, dated the day the phone applies it, and only when Home Assistant's answer differs from the asset's season today. Any other answer changes nothing, and the season card says why. **Follow Home Assistant**, **Force in season** and **Force out of season** choose who decides; **Sync now** checks at once; **Stop syncing** and **Resume syncing** pause the link.

The phone only reads, from the one address you entered, and it checks: on Test connection; on the fresh check that linking, changing a link's entity ID, choosing Follow, resuming, or saving a changed address, token, network option or captured Wi-Fi asks for; on Sync now; when you open or return to ServiceTag and a link has had no successful check within the interval you chose; and in the background at that interval, with **Any network**, or with **Only on this home Wi-Fi** and its background checks On. Choose **Any network** for an https address you have made reachable from outside your home, or **Only on this home Wi-Fi**, where the phone contacts Home Assistant only while it is on the Wi-Fi you captured. **Background checks on this home network** are Off unless you turn them On.

The token is kept encrypted on this phone. It is never in a backup, an export, a merge or a Transfer Pack, never on the Developer API or the MCP, and never logged. The connection and the links belong to this phone too: no ServiceTag backup carries them, and a replace restore removes every link, so you link the assets again. **Disconnect** deletes the connection, every link and the token from the phone; delete the token in Home Assistant as well. The setup, a made-up example and the full limits are in [Home Assistant season sync](../home-assistant-season-sync.md).

## Developer API & MCP

The Developer API and the MCP tools reach everything above but the Home Assistant season sync, which they can only read. New routes and tools list, create, change and archive supply items (`/v1/supply-items`) and set or remove which assets take them (`/v1/asset-supplies`); add, change, remove and replace installed components (`/v1/installed-components`); and read and add a supply item's or an installed component's files and links, the link and file tools taking whichever of the three owners you name. A material line on a quick action, a journal entry or a schedule completion can carry `supplyId`. `GET /v1/assets/{id}/season-sync` and the MCP tool `get_season_sync` show a link's state, without the address, the Wi-Fi's name or the token, and change nothing; linking and the connection stay on the phone. There are now 28 sub-resources under `/v1/assets/{id}/`, and the MCP server has 90 tools. The new tools refuse a phone running an older ServiceTag before sending anything.

**Two things a script could meet:** a quick action or a journal entry sent without `supplyId` on a line clears that line's link, so send back what you read — an MCP server from 1.6.0 or earlier does not: its `save_profile` clears every supply link on any quick-action edit, so update the MCP server with the app; and starting or ending a season, or changing its mode, on an asset whose season follows Home Assistant is refused with 409 `SEASON_SYNC_ENABLED` until you stop syncing on the phone. A link added through `POST /v1/references` without `assetId` is not a third: 1.6.0 refused that body too, and only the message now names the three owners; the MCP tools already send their arguments by name.

## Upgrading

- 1.7.0 installs in place over 1.6.0 and over 1.5.0, and your data stays.
- The database moves to schema 21 and backups to format 20, in one step at first launch. Older backups still import — restore and merge — as before.
- An older ServiceTag, 1.6.0 or earlier, refuses a backup or a Transfer Pack made by this release as too new, rather than dropping what it cannot read: a 1.7.0 export does not restore into 1.6.0 or 1.5.0. Take an export before upgrading.
- Quick actions and journal entries saved before this release name no supply item until you link one; nothing is linked for you.
- What the phone called **Components** now reads **Child assets**; nothing about them changed.
- The Home Assistant season sync stays off until you connect. Location is asked for only if you choose **Only on this home Wi-Fi**, and location all the time only if you also turn its background checks On; **Any network** asks for nothing.

## Known limits

- A supply item or an installed component changed on two phones cannot be merged: the backup or Transfer Pack that carries it, a returning pack included, is refused as a conflict, with no view of the difference.
- Two supply items for the same product are both saved; nothing points out the duplicate.
- Roles are matched exactly: "Oil filter" and "oil filter" are two roles.
- A linked material line looks like any other on the journal; the link shows on the quick action and the entry form.
- Replace asset copies quick actions with their supply links, but not the asset's **Supplies** list or its installed components, which stay with the old asset as its history.
- Replacing an installed component writes no journal entry, and an entry you log for it is not tied to the component.
- A composition has no history of its own: editing it replaces the list.
- A removed installed component keeps its files and links, and the unit that replaced it starts with none.
- An archived supply item keeps its files and links, and you can still add to it from its page, but sharing never offers it.
- The share screen reads installed components and supply items once, when it opens, so one added meanwhile shows the next time you share.
- A supply item's files and links travel in every Transfer Pack whose assets name it, so a large manual makes each such pack larger. Changing one of them while such a pack is out makes that pack's return refused as a conflict; deleting one at home while it is out brings it back with the return.
- Linking an asset to Home Assistant makes its season one started and ended by hand, dated the day you link it: a calendar season's dates are replaced and a year-round asset is in season from that day, so maintenance that counts from the start of the season counts from then. The sheet says so before anything is written.
- **Only on this home Wi-Fi** needs precise location, with Location on: Android shows a Wi-Fi network's name only then. ServiceTag reads the name, never where you are.
- Turning **Background checks on this home network** On asks for location all the time; refused or withdrawn later, the phone checks only when you open ServiceTag and on Sync now.
- On Android 10, turning background checks On goes straight to Android's own permission dialog, without ServiceTag's explanation first: Android 10 gives no name for the option to quote.
- An https Home Assistant whose certificate comes from your own certificate authority is refused: "Home Assistant's certificate could not be verified."
- After Android restores ServiceTag from its own backup, on a new phone or a reinstall, the token is not there: each link says "Enter the access token again: this phone no longer has it." and sends nothing until you do.
- The API and the MCP keep two older names: child assets are still `components` (`/v1/assets/{id}/components`, `create_component`), and a link is still a reference (`/v1/references`) whichever owner it has.

The Developer API's contracts — every route, field and refusal named here — are in [`docs/api/v1.md`](../api/v1.md).
